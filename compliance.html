Legal & Compliance

Understanding mandatory tenancy registration under Section 55 of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act and the Indian Registration Act 1908.

Legal Framework

Why Rent Agreement Registration is Mandatory

Section 55 of Maharashtra Rent Control Act, 1999

According to Section 55(1) of the Maharashtra Rent Control Act, any agreement for a tenancy or leave and license between a landlord and tenant or between a licensor and licensee shall be in writing and shall be registered under the Registration Act, 1908.

The legal responsibility of registering such an agreement rests primarily upon the landlord/licensor. Failure to register can lead to statutory penalties, including fines and imprisonment up to three months under Section 55(2).

Admissibility as Evidence in Court

An unregistered tenancy agreement is inadmissible as primary evidence in a court of law under Section 49 of the Indian Registration Act, 1908. In the event of rental non-payment, unauthorized tenancy holding, or property damages, an unregistered agreement severely weakens the owner's legal standing.

A government e-registered agreement containing UIDAI biometric authentication and registration seal carries absolute evidentiary weight before all judicial and civil authorities.

Key Acts

Applicable Legislative Acts

Our documentation adheres strictly to three fundamental legal statutes.

Registration Act, 1908

Mandates the registration of immovable property leases, establishing legal validity and government archival of public deeds.

Maharashtra Rent Control Act

Defines the obligations of landlords and tenants, security deposit regulations, and formal eviction procedures.

Information Technology Act, 2000

Confers statutory recognition to electronic signatures and biometric records used during e-registration.
